# Bookmarks & Source References

Curated external references for the project. Cartographic style sources for the
Fancy stage are also tracked in `docs/roadmap/fancy-style.md`; this file collects
links and background research.

## Universal Weltatlas / Hansa Weltatlas — Pfahl-Verlag, 7th ed., 1952

The primary general-atlas style reference for the Fancy stage (reference #1 in
`docs/roadmap/fancy-style.md`). Informally referred to in this project as the
"Zimmermann atlas" — the name is justified: the colophon credits **Alfred
Zimmermann, Kartograph, Stuttgart** for the cartographic design and the redesign
of the historical maps.

**Full bibliographic record (from the colophon):**

- **Title:** *Universal Weltatlas — 103 mehrfarbige Haupt- und Nebenkarten nebst
  Geschichtsatlas mit Erläuterungen. Ausführliches alphabetisches Namen- und
  Ortsregister, 30 000 Stichwörter.*
  *(Later editions retitled **Hansa Weltatlas**; contents identical.)*
- **Text editors:** Prof. Dr. Oswald Muris (general section), Dr. Hans Kleinert
  (historical atlas).
- **Cartographic design / historical maps:** Alfred Zimmermann, Kartograph,
  Stuttgart S. (co-cartographer on several editions: **Rudi Hubert**).
- **Copyright:** Hermann Pfahl-Verlag, Laupheim 1950.
- **Series:** Stuttgarter Hausbücherei (a book-club series founded 1948 by Georg
  von Holtzbrinck; Pfahl was the actual publisher).
- **Editions known:** 1949, 1951 (6th), 1952 (7th), 1954 (8th), 1955 (9th), 1960.
- **Edition / year of this copy:** 7th edition, 1952.
- **Extent:** 288 pp.; 103 multi-colour main and inset maps; historical atlas with
  commentary; ~30,000-entry alphabetical place-name index.
- **Physical description:** brown linen binding, gold-embossed spine and cover title.

Listing: [Universal Weltatlas — AbeBooks](https://www.abebooks.com/Universal-Weltatlas-103-mehrfarbige-Haupt--Nebenkarten/31538853360/bd)

**Alfred Zimmermann** — no separate biographical source found beyond the colophon
credit. He appears in antiquarian listings as the named cartographer of the
*Hansa Weltatlas* across multiple 1950s editions, suggesting steady long-term
involvement with Pfahl. No birth/death dates established.

## Serendipity: the actual cartographers named Zimmermann

While chasing the "Zimmermann atlas" name, research turned up two real historical
cartographers named Zimmermann. Neither is the book above — but both are worth
keeping, the first one genuinely relevant to the Fancy-stage relief aesthetic.

### Carl Zimmermann (b. ~1813, active 1839–1845)

Prussian military cartographer; student of the geographer **Carl Ritter** and a
close collaborator of **Alexander von Humboldt**. Born ~1813 (he was 26 in summer
1839 per the source; death year not established in this research pass). As a
Second Lieutenant at the *Allgemeine Kriegsschule* in Berlin he presented Humboldt
with preliminary studies for depicting Central Asia's mountain systems; his work
was published partly in response to Humboldt's *Asie centrale* (1843).

**Key works (1840–1845):** *Übersichtsblatt von Inner-Asien* (1840); *Karte
Inner-Asien's* (1840, four sheets for Ritter's *Allgemeine Erdkunde*); *Entwurf
des Kriegstheaters Russlands gegen Chiwa* (1840); *Denkschrift über den untern
Lauf des Oxus* (1845, 184 pp.). Publishers: Berlin — Reimer, Delius.

**Why it's actually relevant.** His maps fused military topographic survey with
geoscientific data — historical travel routes, ethnography, animal/plant
geography, and explicitly *geomorphological* elements (sand dunes, dried-up river
courses). This early geomorphological cartography is a good reference for relief
that carries meaning rather than decoration (cf. the von Reilly 1791 hachure note
in `docs/roadmap/fancy-style.md`).

Source: [Ulrich Päßler, "Steppenlandschaft in Bewegung" — Visionscarto](https://www.visionscarto.net/steppenlandschaft-in-bewegung)

### Balthasar Zimmermann (1570–1633/34)

Saxon surveyor (Annaberg → Dresden). Succeeded his uncle **Matthias Oeder** on the
*Erste Kursächsische Landesaufnahme* (begun 1586), continuing it at 1:53,333⅓ —
the **"Oeder-Zimmermann"** survey, interrupted by his death in the Thirty Years'
War and unpublished until 1889. Early-modern cadastral survey, not atlas
cartography — kept here purely for the record.

Source: [Balthasar Zimmermann (Kartograf) — Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balthasar_Zimmermann_(Kartograf))
